# Reflexes

Read this once the direction from [visual direction](visual-direction.md) is written, and check the direction, then the build, against it. It loads after the direction because a list of model defaults in view while ideating makes the design timid.

Each item below is what models reach for by default, and the brief's own words can earn any of them. Pick one only for a reason the brief gives; a subject association is not a reason. When one appears on an axis the brief left free, you were not deciding: rewrite the element rather than softening it. These lists never pick the direction: once they hold, spend the page on the committed world. Apply them without announcing them.

## Page shapes

The pages categories always ship: a headline over subcopy and a button with a row of equal cards below; a split hero with copy on one side and product on the other above a badge row; a centered headline over a row of cards. Re-skinned, they are still the template.

## Fonts

On Persuade and Experience surfaces the training-data reflex faces are Inter, Roboto, Geist, Fraunces, Playfair Display, Cormorant, Lora, Crimson, Newsreader, Syne, Space Grotesk, Space Mono, IBM Plex, DM Sans, DM Serif, Outfit, Plus Jakarta Sans, Instrument Sans, and Instrument Serif. Use one only for a reason no other face satisfies; a subject association (books to serif, bookshops to hand lettering, tech to mono) is never that reason.

An oversized italic serif hero (Fraunces, Recoleta, Playfair, Newsreader italic) belongs to an editorial register; elsewhere set the hero headline roman or in a non-serif display face.

## Palettes

Generated interfaces cluster in four looks; where the brief leaves the aesthetic free, stay out of each:

- a warm cream ground with a high-contrast serif display and a terracotta or signal-red accent;
- near-black with one neon accent and glowing edges;
- broadsheet hairlines with an italic display serif and small tracked mono labels;
- flat saturated ink with black boxes, hard offset shadows, and a heavy condensed display.

Claude's measured prior renders warm, bookish, family, and child-facing subjects as a cream ground with serif display, italic accents, and lamplight even when nobody asked; dark pages also tend to drift to blue-black slate. Treat that first palette as spent: such a subject does not license cream. A near-white warm ground (roughly OKLCH L 0.84–0.97, chroma under 0.06, hue 40–100) reads as cream whatever its token is called. Rework an unpinned palette that reads as cream, paper, parchment, ivory, or lamplight from the world's saturated materials (book cloth, thread, jackets, endpapers), and carry warmth in the accent, type, and imagery. Violet-to-purple gradients, and cyan joined by a second neon hue on near-black, are the same reflex unless the brand documents them.

## Composition

- A page built from same-size cards of an icon tile, a heading, and text. Set an icon beside its heading or in the flow.
- A card nested inside a card. An inner region that scrolls or acts on its own takes a tint or a divider, not a second frame.
- The hero-metric template: a big number, a small label, supporting stats, and an accent.
- A small tracked uppercase label or pill above a heading. Fold its words into the heading or the body; when they are navigation, make them a breadcrumb or a link. An established system that uses these labels keeps them.
- Section numbers, unless the sequence carries information the reader needs.

## Surfaces

- Gradient-filled text. Take emphasis from weight or size.
- Glass and blur as decoration rather than as a specific effect.
- A colored stripe thicker than 1px on one side, the top, or the bottom of a rounded card, list item, callout, or alert. A square-edged rule on a blockquote, timeline entry, or table-row marker is an older convention and stays.
- Shadows without offset, such as a colored halo or a glow on dark. Give shadows an offset and a soft blur.
- Zero-blur hard offset shadows, outside a world that actually chose neobrutalism.
- A hairline border and a wide soft shadow (32px blur or more) together on every card in a row of three or more. Declare elevation once; a single floating panel may carry both.
- A saturated radial glow fading to transparent behind a hero or section, unless it carries lighting, focus, or spatial meaning.
- Repeating stripes or grid-line backgrounds, unless the surface is a canvas, map, blueprint, chart, editor, or measuring tool. Texture comes from the subject's world.
- Sparklines, progress rings, or soft rounded rectangles standing in for content. Real history on a changing number is content.
- Faked physicality: bevels, embossing, stamped metal, or chalk imitating a material the page never renders.
- A pulsing dot not tied to live, changing data, or a blinking caret in text nobody can edit.

## Copy

- A section ending on a short line of manufactured contrast; allow it once per page at most.
- Naming a concept and ironizing it. Say plainly what a thing does or fails to do.
- Em dashes beyond about one per 500 characters. Rephrase the sentence rather than swapping in other punctuation.
